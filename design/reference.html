Shared UI reference

Decided in the UI review on 2026-11-16. The rules beside each piece are short; ui-rules.md has them in full. Samples are in German; every text exists in English too.

Frame and navigation

  • One bar at the top of every app: app name at the start, at the end the "Alle Apps" mark that leads back to the landing page.
  • Main navigation has 2 to 5 fixed places. Under 900 px it is a bar fixed to the bottom (icon over label); from 900 px the same links sit in the top bar as text. More than 5 places: the fifth is "Mehr".
  • A list the person grows (categories) or an app's sub-sections is a side list: a 240 px column from 900 px, a drawer under 900 px, opened by the menu button at the bar's start.
  • Bar content and page content share one left edge and one width (1080 px). Forms and running text stay 720 px wide inside it.
  • The drawer closes on Escape, on the backdrop and after a choice; focus returns to the menu button; nothing behind it can be reached.

Page header

  • Optional back link above the title: a chevron and the name of the place it leads to, 44 px high. No "Zurück zu".
  • One h1 per page, in every state (loading, error, empty). 24 px under 900 px, 28 px from 900 px.
  • At most one main button beside the title: the page's add action. The browser title is "Seite · App".

Buttons and links

  • One height (44 px), one radius (10 px), weight 600. Width follows the text; the main button of a form is full width under 480 px.
  • Main (filled): one per view or form. Never a selected state, never repeated per row.
  • Secondary (outlined): everything else. Quiet (text only): the least important action of a row.
  • Destructive (red outline): only for what loses data, and it always opens a confirmation. The confirming button is filled red.
  • Order: main action first, "Abbrechen" after it. A disabled button looks disabled, and the reason stands beside it.
  • A link that stands alone is 44 px high. Small links exist only inside a sentence.
  • Up to four options that must stay on one line: .segmented.segmented-equal, equal columns, no wrap. A label breaks at a space; a single word breaks only at a soft hyphen (&shy;) the app writes into it, and the pill's radius is half the one-line height. Five or more use the plain .segmented.

Form fields

  • Label above the field, hint below it. Field text is 16 px, never smaller (smaller text makes iPhones zoom).
  • Optional fields say "(optional)". Everything else is required; there is no star.
  • Validation: the button stays enabled. On submit the message appears under its field in the person's words, the field gets a red border, and focus moves to the first wrong field. No browser bubbles, no raw server sentences.
  • Short related fields share one line where the row has room for two fields (two times 9rem and the gap, about 300 px); below that they stack (amount and unit).
  • A form that opens in place replaces the button that opened it, scrolls into view and takes focus; closing returns focus to that button.
  • Fields that answer one question together: fieldset.field-group with a legend; "(optional)" stands once, on the legend.

List rows

  • A list is one box with divider lines. A row: title, at most one muted line, the figure or date at the end.
  • A row that leads somewhere is one link: the whole row is the target (at least 56 px high).
  • A row that picks its thing instead of leading somewhere is button.row; its name is its visible text. Facts that are not things with actions are a .facts list, never .list.
  • A row shows at most one action; the others are behind "…" (edit first, delete last).
  • A row that shows a text action is .row.row-wrap; its action and "…" sit in .row-actions, the row's last child. Under 900 px they stand on a second line, at the row's end; from 900 px the row is one line. .row-end never holds a button.
  • What belongs to a row but does not fit its line (a text, a part that opens, a form, a message) stands in .row-below, a block under the row in the same list item. It has the row's side padding and lines up with the title. Alone in its item it also has the row's padding above. The row menu stays outside it.
  • A preview of longer text may end after two lines. A value is never cut. A long name without a space breaks inside the row; a chip beside it stays on one line.
  • Default order by name; items without data last. Tables scroll in their own box, never the page.

Cards

  • A card groups what belongs together under one heading. Cards do not sit inside cards.
  • A suggestion from the app is tinted, gives its reason and changes nothing until accepted.
  • A card that is one link is clickable as a whole.

Chips and view switches

  • A chip states a fact about a row. Its colour carries the state, its text says it: neutral, positive, warning, error, muted (past or cancelled).
  • Choosing one of several views or filters: pills in a row. The selected one is tinted with an accent border, never filled, so it cannot be mistaken for the main button.
  • Chips and pills wrap with a gap. A chip stays on one line. One that holds a name a person typed also carries .chip-wrap: it is never wider than its container and may break its text.

Messages

  • A message appears where its cause is: a load error in place of the content, a save error under the form's buttons, "Gespeichert" beside the control. No toasts, no bar stuck to the window.
  • Colour plus a mark plus words; colour is never the only carrier.
  • Problems in a live preview are quiet status lines, never alerts.

Empty, loading, error

  • The page keeps its header and navigation in every state.
  • Empty: what is empty, one sentence on what to do, the add button. If the add button stands on the heading line directly above the block, it is not repeated inside.
  • Loading: grey lines in the shape of the content; "Wird geladen …" for screen readers. Old content stays on screen during a reload.
  • Load error: what could not be loaded, what to do, always "Erneut versuchen". The technical detail is small. Never the empty text.
  • Not found, not allowed: the same block with a link to the app's start. No dead ends.

Confirmation

  • What cannot come back asks first, in place of the row or under the button: the question names the thing and the consequence; the confirming button repeats the verb.
  • A question that is not a deletion is .confirm.confirm-neutral: the same order, a neutral border, the accepting button .btn-primary named after the action.
  • What can come back (archive, trash, untick) happens at once.
  • An overlay only when the person must answer before anything else: leaving unsaved changes, a save conflict. It is a native dialog: focus stays inside, Escape cancels.

Dates and numbers

  • Dates and numbers follow the language chosen in the app, not the browser's, through one function per kind.
  • Three date shapes: short (same year), medium (the default), long (a day's heading). English uses day first and the 24-hour clock.
  • A no-break space stands between number and unit. Minus is the minus sign (−), not a hyphen. A comparison is said in words, not with a sign.
  • A field shows a number the way the screen shows it ("68,50"), and accepts comma and point.

Shared wording

  • One word per action in every app, capitalised as a label. German addresses the person as "du".
  • A button names the thing when a page has several forms ("Wiegung speichern"); otherwise the bare verb.
  • Errors say what happened and what to do, without codes and without "leider".
